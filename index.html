📝 Workplace Email Writing Tips
💼

Key Principles for Professional Emails

Noun Usage: Use possessive forms (-'s or -s') correctly to show ownership. Ensure noun number (singular/plural) matches the context and verbs.
Verb Consistency: Maintain subject-verb agreement (e.g., third-person singular subjects require verbs with -s/-es). Use base verbs after infinitives ("to + verb") and modal verbs (would, should, can).
Preposition Collocations: Learn fixed preposition phrases (e.g., "owing to" instead of random prepositions). Prepositions often depend on the words they follow.
Article Rules: Omit articles ("the", "a/an") before proper nouns like festivals, names, or unique events. Use articles appropriately for common nouns.
Pronoun Completeness: Include necessary pronouns to avoid sentence fragments or ambiguity. Ensure pronouns clearly refer to their antecedents.
Spelling & Capitalisation: Double-check common spelling mistakes (e.g., compound words as one word). Capitalise proper nouns, start of sentences, and formal titles.
Conciseness: Keep sentences clear and direct. Avoid redundant words or overly complex structures in professional communication.
Formality: Maintain a polite and professional tone. Use appropriate phrases for requests, thanks, or updates (e.g., "we would appreciate it if" instead of informal expressions).
Clarity: Organise your ideas logically. Start with a clear purpose, provide necessary details, and end with a specific call to action if needed.
Grammar Accuracy: Avoid common errors like subject-verb disagreement, incorrect prepositions, or missing pronouns. Proofread before sending professional emails.
